The short version

  • We can't read encrypted files. The key stays in the link, after the #, and never reaches us.
  • We store very little, briefly: the encrypted file, its size, its expiry time and a hash of its delete code, for 24 hours at most.
  • IP addresses are processed by our host, Cloudflare, to deliver the site, keep it secure and limit uploads. We don't store them with files.
  • No accounts, no analytics, no ads, no tracking cookies. We don't sell or share personal information.
  • Two outside companies at most: Cloudflare, which runs our infrastructure, and Google, only when a LiteShot user asks Google Lens to search a screenshot.

You can reach us about privacy at privacy@fortefile.com.

How a fortefile link protects you

A fortefile link looks like fortefile.com/f/{file ID}#{key}. The file ID tells our server which encrypted file to send. The key, after the #, is the link's “fragment”: browsers keep it on the device and leave it out of every request they send to servers. The uploading app encrypts the file with that key (AES-256-GCM) before uploading it, and the recipient's browser decrypts it after downloading it. We only ever handle encrypted bytes.

Processed when you connect

Like any website, your device sends request data when you visit fortefile.com, upload or download: your IP address, the address requested (never the part after the #), your browser's user agent and other standard headers, and the time. Cloudflare processes this data on our behalf to route and deliver the request, to protect the Service against attacks, and to apply our upload limit of 30 uploads per minute per IP address (or per /64 block for IPv6). Our own code uses the IP address only for that limit, and doesn't store it. The limit's counters reset every minute.

Logs

Our code doesn't log requests, file contents, keys, links or IP addresses. It writes only error messages and the number of files removed by each hourly cleanup. Cloudflare keeps its own operational and security logs, and shows us aggregate traffic statistics and security events, such as requests its firewall blocked, which can include IP addresses. We don't export or keep Cloudflare's request logs.

When you contact us

If you email us, we receive your email address and whatever you include, for example the link in an abuse report. We use it only to reply and to act on your message.

Cookies and similar technologies

We don't set cookies and our pages don't store anything in your browser. If Cloudflare's bot protection needs to check a visitor, Cloudflare may set a strictly necessary security cookie (such as __cf_bm or cf_clearance). These cookies don't track you across websites, and we don't use them for anything else.

What we don't collect

  • Encryption keys or the contents of encrypted files.
  • File names. Apps don't send them, and the server has nowhere to put them.
  • Accounts, names, email addresses or passwords (unless you email us).
  • Analytics, usage tracking, advertising identifiers, browser fingerprints or precise location.
  • Anything from third-party scripts: our pages load nothing from any other website, which our Content Security Policy enforces.

How long we keep data

  • Encrypted files, their details and the delete-code hash: until the file expires (24 hours at most) or is deleted. Expired files stop being available immediately and are erased at the next hourly cleanup. As a backstop, our storage is set to remove anything older than one day. Deleted data can take a short time to be purged from Cloudflare's systems.
  • Public search images: one hour at most. Browsers and other services may keep cached copies for up to five minutes. Google keeps what Google Lens fetched under Google's own policies.
  • Rate-limit counters: about a minute.
  • Cloudflare's logs: kept by Cloudflare under its own retention policies.
  • Emails to us: as long as we need them to deal with the matter, then deleted, unless the law requires us to keep them longer or we need them to establish or defend legal claims.
  • Material reported to NCMEC: preserved for the period U.S. law requires (currently one year).

International transfers

Devgm is based in the United States, and Cloudflare runs a global network, so data may be processed in the United States and in other countries where Cloudflare operates, including when you connect from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland. Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies, these transfers are covered by Cloudflare's Data Processing Addendum, which includes the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK International Data Transfer Addendum, and by Cloudflare's certification under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and its UK and Swiss extensions. Encrypted files stay encrypted wherever they are stored.

How to make a request

Email privacy@fortefile.com. You may use an authorized agent, who will need to show that you gave them permission. Please understand what's possible: we hold very little data, none of it tied to a name or account, and we can't link a file to a person. If your request concerns a particular file, include its link (the part before the # is enough to identify it). If you want a file removed and no longer have the app that uploaded it, send us the link and we'll delete it. We may ask for information that shows the request is yours, and if we can't identify you in our data, we'll tell you. We reply within one month (45 days under U.S. state laws) and may extend that where the law allows.

fortefile is not directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has given us personal information, contact privacy@fortefile.com and we'll delete it.

Security

Files are encrypted on the uploader's device with AES-256-GCM and a new random 256-bit key. Every connection uses HTTPS with HSTS. Delete codes are stored only as SHA-256 hashes and checked in constant time. File IDs are random and can't be guessed. Our pages run under a strict Content Security Policy with no third-party code, and uploaded files are served so they can never run as a web page on our domain. Everything is deleted within a day.

No system is perfectly secure. Our security pledge describes our measures in detail, what fortefile can't protect against, and how to report a vulnerability. If a security incident affects personal data, we'll notify the authorities and affected people as the law requires. Because we don't know who our users are, we'll also post a notice on fortefile.com.

Law enforcement and legal requests

fortefile is designed so that there is very little we could hand over, even if we wanted to.

What we can provide, only while a file still exists (24 hours at most) and only in response to valid legal process:

  • the encrypted bytes of a file, which can't be read without the key;
  • its size, content type, whether it is encrypted, and its upload and expiry times;
  • the SHA-256 hash of its delete code;
  • for a public search image, the image itself, during the hour it exists.

What we can't provide, because we never have it: encryption keys or decrypted content; file names; the name, account, email address or IP address of whoever uploaded or opened a file; or files that have expired or been deleted. The one exception is material we preserve because the law requires it: we keep that file and, if the person who reported it sent us the full link, its key.

  • How to send a request. Email legal@fortefile.com. We require valid legal process under U.S. law, such as a subpoena, court order or search warrant, and we review every request for legal sufficiency and scope. Authorities outside the United States should use a mutual legal assistance treaty or another appropriate channel.
  • Preservation. We honor valid preservation requests (18 U.S.C. § 2703(f)) for data that still exists when we receive them. Files expire within 24 hours, so requests must be prompt.
  • Emergencies. We may disclose information we hold if we believe in good faith that an emergency involving danger of death or serious physical injury requires it.
  • Child safety. We report apparent child sexual abuse material to NCMEC as required by 18 U.S.C. § 2258A, and preserve what we report as the law requires. See our Child Safety Policy.
  • Notice to users. There are no accounts, so we generally have no way to tell a user about a request.
