The service

fortefile keeps files for a short time and lets anyone who has a file's link download it.

  • Encrypted files. Apps that upload to fortefile, such as LiteShot, encrypt each file on the uploader's device with AES-256-GCM before sending it. The key is placed after the “#” in the link. Browsers don't send that part of a link to servers, so the key never reaches us. The fortefile viewer decrypts the file in the recipient's browser.
  • Public search images. When a LiteShot user chooses “Search similar images”, that screenshot is uploaded without encryption, as a PNG or JPEG image with a public link, so that Google Lens can fetch it. These images are deleted within one hour.
  • Limits. Files can be up to 25 MB (25 MiB). Each file is kept for 1, 12 or 24 hours, as the uploader chooses, and never longer than 24 hours. We limit how many uploads can come from one network address in a short time.
  • Free, with no accounts. fortefile costs nothing and has no accounts. If we ever charge for something, we'll tell you first, and separate terms will apply.

fortefile is a way to share files for a short time. It is not a storage or backup service, so keep your own copy of anything you upload. We don't promise that the Service will be available at any particular time, and we may change, limit or stop all or part of it.

We can't see encrypted files

fortefile is designed so that we don't know what's in encrypted files. We receive only encrypted bytes, and never the key. As a result:

  • we can't view, scan, moderate or recover the contents of an encrypted file, unless someone gives us its full link, including the part after the “#”;
  • we can't decrypt a file for you, for anyone else, or for any authority;
  • we can't tell who uploaded a file, because there are no accounts and we don't store network addresses with files.

Because we can't review files in advance, we don't monitor content and we don't endorse it. We act on reports, as described below. You, not Devgm, are responsible for the content you upload.

Retention and deletion

  • Automatic expiry. Every file expires 1, 12 or 24 hours after upload, as the uploader chose. Public search images expire within one hour. From the moment a file expires its link stops working, and the file is erased from storage at the next hourly cleanup. As a safety net, our storage is also set to remove anything older than one day.
  • Early deletion. The uploading app can delete a file at any time with its delete code (in LiteShot, from the tray menu). Deletion takes effect immediately, for everyone.
  • Our deletions. We may delete a file or block access to it at any time if we believe it breaks these Terms or the law, if we receive a valid notice or legal order, or to protect the Service or other people.
  • No recovery. Expired and deleted files can't be restored. Public search images may stay in browser and other caches for up to five minutes, and our providers can take a short time to purge deleted data from their systems.
