Child Safety Policy

fortefile has zero tolerance for child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and for anything that sexually exploits or endangers children. This policy explains what's prohibited, what we do about it and how to report it. It's part of our Terms of Service.

The short version

  • Zero tolerance. No child sexual abuse material on fortefile, in any form, including drawn, computer-generated and AI-generated images.
  • Report it, never share it. Send only the link to abuse@fortefile.com, or report straight to NCMEC's CyberTipline. Don't download, screenshot or forward the material.
  • We act at once. We disable the link, report it to NCMEC, preserve it as the law requires and block the networks involved.

What's prohibited

Don't upload, share, ask for or link to any of the following. A minor is anyone under 18. This applies to every file on fortefile, encrypted or not, whether or not you share the link, and whatever the law says where you are.

  • Child sexual abuse material. Any photo, video or other visual depiction of a minor engaged in sexually explicit conduct, as defined in 18 U.S.C. § 2256, or of the sexual abuse of a child.
  • Generated and altered images. Drawn, animated, computer-generated or AI-generated sexual depictions of minors, and real images edited or “deepfaked” to sexualize a child, whether or not a real child is shown.
  • Sexualization of minors. Content that presents children in a sexual way, even without nudity, or that is collected or shared out of sexual interest in children.
  • Grooming and sextortion. Content used to groom, pressure or blackmail a minor, to arrange sexual contact with one, or to threaten to share a minor's intimate images.
  • Trafficking. Content that advertises, solicits or helps arrange the sexual exploitation or trafficking of children.
  • Pointers and promotion. Links to this material elsewhere, guides to finding, making or hiding it, and content that promotes, normalizes or celebrates the sexual abuse of children.

How fortefile limits abuse

Most fortefile files are encrypted on the uploader's device, and the key never reaches us. That protects everyone's privacy, and it also means we can't scan encrypted files for known abuse material the way services that can read their files do. We won't claim otherwise, and we won't weaken the encryption. Instead, fortefile is built to be a poor tool for abuse:

  • every link stops working within 24 hours, and public search images within one hour;
  • files are limited to 25 MiB, and uploads are rate-limited for each network;
  • there are no accounts, profiles, folders, search or public listings, so files can't be browsed or discovered;
  • we can block IP addresses and networks that abuse the service;
  • anyone who has a link can report it, and a report with the full link lets us see that one file.

What we do when we find it

Reports of child sexual abuse material come before everything else we do. When we learn of apparent CSAM on fortefile, we:

  1. Disable it. The link stops working for everyone, straight away. We don't warn the uploader, and the file is never restored.
  2. Report it. We report it to the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC) through its CyberTipline, as 18 U.S.C. § 2258A requires. NCMEC makes reports available to law enforcement in the United States and around the world.
  3. Preserve it. We keep the reported material and the information we hold about it outside the 24-hour deletion schedule, locked away from everyone else, for as long as U.S. law requires (currently one year), and longer where the law allows and law enforcement asks us to.
  4. Block it. We block the IP addresses and networks involved where we can, and take any other step that helps stop the material spreading.
  5. Cooperate. We respond to valid legal process from law enforcement, as described below.

We look at a reported file only as far as we need to confirm what it is and report it. Because fortefile keeps so little, what we can pass on is usually limited: see what we can and can't provide.

How to report

Never download, save, screenshot, copy or forward the material itself, even to report it. Possessing or sharing it is a serious crime, whatever the intention. Send the link only.

  • Tell us. Email abuse@fortefile.com with “Child safety” in the subject and the full fortefile link, including everything after the “#”. Without that part we can't see the file. You can report anonymously.
  • Tell NCMEC. Report directly to NCMEC's CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org or 1-800-843-5678. Outside the United States, use a national hotline listed by INHOPE, or in the UK the Internet Watch Foundation.
  • A child in immediate danger: call your local emergency number (911 in the United States) first.

Files expire within 24 hours, so please report as soon as you can.

If it's happening to you

If someone is threatening to share sexual images of you, or pressuring you to send them, it's not your fault and you're not in trouble. Stop replying, don't pay, keep the messages and tell an adult you trust.

  • Report it to NCMEC's CyberTipline at report.cybertip.org.
  • If the images were taken before you turned 18, NCMEC's Take It Down service can help stop them being shared online, without the images leaving your device.
  • Send us the fortefile link, and we'll take it down.

Law enforcement

Send legal process and preservation requests to legal@fortefile.com. Our Privacy Policy explains what we need and what we can provide. In an emergency involving danger of death or serious physical injury to a child, put “Emergency” in the subject line: we may disclose information without legal process where U.S. law allows (18 U.S.C. § 2702).
